Round 2How the chance is calculated

The model never sees names or reputations. It turns 40 numbers about the two fighters into a chance, in four steps.

  1. Measure

    40 numbers known before fight night, mostly gaps between the two men: 4 years of age, 55 Elo points, 3 more takedown attempts per 15 minutes for Salkilld.

  2. Ask the trees

    950 small decision trees each ask a few yes-or-no questions about those gaps, such as how wide the age gap is, and nudge a score up or down.

  3. Add and average

    The nudges add up to one score. Eight versions of the model, with each fighter tried in both corners, are averaged: -0.73, where minus means towards Salkilld. Zero would be dead even.

  4. Turn it into a chance

    An S-shaped curve turns the score into a percentage, tuned on past fights so its percentages come true as often as they say: -0.73 becomes 68% for Salkilld.

How firm is 68%?

The eight versions of the model barely disagree here (Salkilld 67–68%), but all eight learn from the same 6,276 fights. A sterner test retrains the model 40 times, each time on a random redraw of those fights, so some count twice and others drop out. The middle 36 answers run from 60% to 74%. All 40 still pick Salkilld, so the pick holds, but the exact number is softer than one figure suggests.

Round 5The fight, as the numbers see it

Early read, three months out · built only from the model and the fight data

Two finishers. Every one of Saint Denis' nine UFC wins ended inside the distance, and Salkilld has finished five of his six, the last four in the first round. They share an opponent: Saint Denis knocked out Beneil Dariush in 16 seconds in November 2025, and Salkilld stopped him in the first round six months later. Both also won on the same Sydney card, UFC 325, in January.

Saint Denis brings the heavier top game. He spends 34% of his fight time in control (top 6% of active men) and tries 0.88 submissions per 15 minutes (top 3%), and he lands 55% of his significant strikes. His four UFC losses: Elizeu Zaleski dos Santos on the judges' cards in his debut, then Dustin Poirier, Renato Moicano and, in July, Paddy Pimblett, who submitted him in 52 seconds.

Salkilld is the busier wrestler and the harder target. His takedown rate, 11.1 attempts per 15 minutes, is in the top 1% of active men, and head strikes land on him 40% of the time against 45% for Saint Denis. The catch is the sample: his six UFC fights add up to 28 minutes 45 seconds of fighting, against 87 minutes over 13 fights for Saint Denis, so his numbers are smoothed more strongly towards the league average.

Five rounds are new ground for both. Neither man has fought past 15 minutes in the UFC. Salkilld's one main event ended in the first round, a finish of Mateusz Gamrot in August; Saint Denis' one main event ended in defeat, stopped by Renato Moicano after two rounds. At 27 against 31, Salkilld also has the model's biggest single factor on his side.

The readSalkilld, on age, defence and the stronger recent wins. Saint Denis' path runs through his accuracy and his top game, and his sharper striking is the main reason the model stops at two chances in three.

After the final bellWhat the model can't see

  • Five roundsIt does not know this is a 25-minute main event. Neither man has fought past 15 minutes in the UFC.
  • Home crowdSalkilld is Australian and fights in Sydney. The model does not use where a fight takes place.
  • Fights outside the UFCIt sees only UFC fights: 6 for Salkilld, 13 for Saint Denis. Their full professional records (Salkilld 13-1, Saint Denis 17-4) include fights it never sees.
  • Fight weekWeight cuts, training camps and injuries are not in the data, and the fight is three months away.
