Age is the single biggest factor. The six-year gap is worth about 11 points to the younger man, twice Prates' whole Elo edge. Elo is Prates' best argument: it measures who you beat, not just whether you won. His last two wins were knockouts of former champions Leon Edwards and Jack Della Maddalena, and the rating counts a finish as a full win, so his Elo sits 62 points above Morales'. Defence is Morales' second reason: head strikes land on him 37% of the time and leg strikes 75%, against 40% and 80% for Prates.

Round 2How the chance is calculated

The model never sees names or reputations. It turns 40 numbers about the two fighters into a chance, in four steps.

  1. Measure

    40 numbers known before fight night, mostly gaps between the two men: 6 years of age, 62 Elo points, 39 more significant strikes thrown per 15 minutes by Morales.

  2. Ask the trees

    950 small decision trees each ask a few yes-or-no questions about those gaps, such as how wide the age gap is, and nudge a score up or down.

  3. Add and average

    The nudges add up to one score. Eight versions of the model, with each fighter tried in both corners, are averaged: +0.70 towards Morales. Zero would be dead even.

  4. Turn it into a chance

    An S-shaped curve turns the score into a percentage, tuned on past fights so its percentages come true as often as they say: +0.70 becomes 67%.

How firm is 67%?

The eight versions of the model barely disagree here (Morales 66–67%), but all eight learn from the same 6,276 fights. A sterner test retrains the model 40 times, each time on a random redraw of those fights, so some count twice and others drop out. The middle 36 answers run from 59% to 69%. All 40 still pick Morales, so the pick holds, but the exact number is softer than one figure suggests.

Round 5The fight, as the numbers see it

Early read, three months out · built only from the model and the fight data

Two finishers who stay on their feet. Neither man wrestles: Prates tries 1.1 takedowns per 15 minutes and Morales 2.0, against a typical 3.7, and both stop about three in four of the takedowns tried on them. Neither has been knocked down in the UFC, and both have stopped Trevin Giles and Neil Magny.

Prates hits harder, and from further out. He has scored a knockdown in all seven of his UFC wins, 12 in all, and his rate of 0.97 per 15 minutes is the highest of any active man. At range he lands 50% to Morales' 45%. His last two wins were knockouts of former champions: Leon Edwards at UFC 322, the night Morales stopped Sean Brady, and Jack Della Maddalena in May.

Morales throws more and gets hit less. He averages 161 significant strikes per 15 minutes (top 7% of active men) to Prates' 122, and head strikes land on him 37% of the time against 40% for Prates. His last three fights, against Magny, Gilbert Burns and Brady, were first-round stoppages that lasted 11 minutes 45 seconds in total.

The distance is the open question. Morales has never fought past the third round in the UFC; his longest fights are two 15-minute decisions from 2023. Prates has headlined twice, both times booked for five rounds: he lost the one that went 25 minutes, outlanded 126 to 63 by Ian Machado Garry, and stopped Della Maddalena in the third. At 27 against 33, Morales also has the model's biggest single factor on his side.

The readMorales, on youth, volume and defence. Prates' path is a clean shot: he has knocked down every man he has beaten in the UFC, and his wins over stronger opposition are why the model stops at two chances in three.

After the final bellWhat the model can't see

  • Five roundsIt does not know this is a 25-minute title fight. Morales has never been past the third round in the UFC, and Prates' only 25-minute fight was his only UFC loss.
  • Fights outside the UFCIt sees only UFC fights: 8 for Prates, 7 for Morales. Their full professional records (Prates 24-7, Morales 19-0) include many fights it never sees.
  • Fight weekWeight cuts, training camps and injuries are not in the data, and the fight is three months away.

This is an experiment in what a model built from public fight statistics can say about one fight. Treat it as one informed opinion: in fights where it was this sure, it has been right about seven times in ten.
