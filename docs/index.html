How it gets to a prediction

The model never sees names or reputations, only numbers built from each fighter's UFC history. Every fight goes through the same six steps.

  1. Build the inputsEvery UFC fight since 1994 is turned into 40 numbers known before fight night, mostly gaps between the two fighters: age, reach, an Elo rating of who each has beaten (a finish counts more than a split decision), and striking, wrestling and defence averaged over the last 8 fights. Short records are pulled towards the league average.40 inputs per fight, 6,276 fights
  2. Ask the trees950 small decision trees, each asking at most four yes-or-no questions such as “is the age gap over 1.5 years?”, add their nudges into one score. A curve turns the score into a chance.950 trees, at most 4 questions each
  3. Swap the cornersThe fight is asked again with the fighters swapped and the two answers are averaged, so the result can't depend on who is listed first.asked one way only, an early version changed its pick in 35% of fights
  4. Average eight versionsEight copies of the model, each trained with a different random seed, are averaged to remove the luck of one draw.8 versions × 2 corners = 16 answers per fight
  5. CalibrateOne stretch factor, fitted on past fights, makes the percentages come true as often as they say (the table above).stretch 1.235: a raw 70% becomes 74%
  6. ExplainSHAP splits the prediction into one push per input, so every pick comes with its reasons.the pushes add up exactly to the prediction

The test above used a model trained on 2014–2024. The previews use the same frozen recipe retrained on all 6,276 fights from 2014 to October 2026.

What the model can't see

  • Fights outside the UFCIt reads only UFC fights. A fighter's earlier record elsewhere is invisible, and a fighter with no UFC fight can't be predicted at all.
  • Five roundsIt doesn't know whether a fight is scheduled for 15 or 25 minutes, or whether a title is at stake.
  • Where the fight isHome crowd, travel and time zones are not inputs.
  • Fight weekWeight cuts, training camps, injuries and late replacements are not in the data.
  • Matchups head-onEvery input is a gap between the two fighters, so the model compares them skill by skill. It never sets one man's attack directly against the other's defence.

Treat each pick as one informed opinion. On the 643 test fights without a debutant, when it gave the favourite under 60% the favourite won 57% of the time; at 60–70%, 66%; above 70%, 72%.
