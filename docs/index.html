How it gets to a prediction

The model never sees names or reputations, only numbers built from each fighter's UFC history. The worked example is UFC 333, Volkanovski vs Evloev, which the model scored at 58–42 for Evloev. Volkanovski won by TKO in round 2.

  1. Build the inputsEvery UFC fight since 1994 is turned into 40 numbers known before fight night, mostly gaps between the two fighters: age, reach, an Elo rating of who each has beaten (a finish counts more than a split decision), and striking, wrestling and defence averaged over the last 8 fights. Short records are pulled towards the league average.Evloev 6 years younger, Elo 119 points lower
  2. Ask the trees950 small decision trees, each asking at most four yes-or-no questions such as “is the age gap over 1.5 years?”, add their nudges into one score. A curve turns the score into a chance.Evloev 49.8%
  3. Swap the cornersThe fight is asked again with the fighters swapped and the two answers are averaged, so the result can't depend on who is listed first.Evloev 56.4%
  4. Average eight versionsEight copies of the model, each trained with a different random seed, are averaged to remove the luck of one draw.Evloev 56.4%
  5. CalibrateOne stretch factor, fitted on past fights, makes the percentages come true as often as they say (the table above).Evloev 57.9%
  6. ExplainSHAP splits the prediction into one push per input, so every pick comes with its reasons.Age +12 pts Evloev, Elo +9 pts Volkanovski

The test above used a model trained on 2014–2024. The previews use the same frozen recipe retrained on all 6,276 fights from 2014 to October 2026.

What the model can't see

  • Fights outside the UFCIt reads only UFC fights. A fighter's earlier record elsewhere is invisible, and a fighter with no UFC fight can't be predicted at all.
  • Five roundsIt doesn't know whether a fight is scheduled for 15 or 25 minutes, or whether a title is at stake.
  • Where the fight isHome crowd, travel and time zones are not inputs.
  • Fight weekWeight cuts, training camps, injuries and late replacements are not in the data.
  • Matchups head-onEvery input is a gap between the two fighters, so the model compares them skill by skill. It never sets one man's attack directly against the other's defence.

Treat each pick as one informed opinion. On the 812 test fights, when it gave the favourite under 60% the favourite won 56% of the time; at 60–70%, 66%; above 70%, 73%.
